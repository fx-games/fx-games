Of Heaven and Hell

Started January 2024 · Go, Ebitengine, WebAssembly · tile roguelike

Completion ▰▰▰▰▰▰▰▱▱▱ 70% · D1 mode finished, own design still pending

▶ Play in browser

Of Heaven and Hell is my love letter to Angband and the original Diablo: a top-down, turn-based, grid-based dungeon crawler with procedurally generated levels. It won't win any innovation awards, and that was never the point. I wanted that Angband feeling with a modern UI and quality-of-life features, in runs you can enjoy in a short session.

Under the hood it's Go and Ebitengine, reusing bits from my older projects. You get line of sight, pathfinding, a class and skill system, inventory and equipment, loot, quests and unique monsters. I simplified a few classic rules on purpose: there's no to-hit roll, so attacks just connect unless something flies or dodges. There are no damage types either, only armor, and ranged weapons never run out of ammo.

Lately most of my time has gone into the engine side. Content lives in data-driven mods, there's a Diablo-flavoured level set, and the web build runs as WebAssembly with resizable, rearrangeable windows for the map, inventory, messages and so on. Press F1 in-game for help.

Features

  • Turn-based grid combat on an energy clock: every action costs time, and in the D1 mod a sword swing costs two steps, straight from Diablo's animation frame counts.
  • Data-driven mods: the original H&H content and a turn-based Diablo 1 rebuild run on the same engine.
  • Four dungeon types (Cathedral, Catacombs, Caves, Hell), 16 levels plus set levels, and a town with Griswold, Adria, Pepin, Cain and Wirt.
  • All 26 single-player spells, with books, scrolls and staves.
  • Quests with set pieces (Butcher, Leoric, Zhar, Warlord, Lazarus) and Diablo on level 16, followed by an end screen.
  • Durability, identification, shrines, chests, barrels and fountains.
  • Light and line of sight: monsters see your torch and hear you nearby.
  • Save slots, with optional permadeath.
  • Graphical tiles, plus an ASCII terminal mode in the desktop build.
  • An autoplay bot that plays runs to measure pacing, which you can also watch.

Under the hood

≈ 68,200 lines of Go · 8,000 lines of Go tests · 356 commits

The whole Diablo timing model fits on one line. Animation ticks become game time, so D1's walk-to-attack ratio holds in a turn-based game (src/common/hero.go):

func ticksToAUT(ticks int) int { return (ticks*10 + rules.RefWalkTicks/2) / rules.RefWalkTicks }

Ideas & inspirations

Angband and Diablo, all the way down. The README starts with "This won't win any awards in terms of innovation anytime soon, I know." docs/D1_PLAN.md sets the later goal: "play through the complete Diablo 1 game as a turn-based variant."

The early notes go elsewhere. docs/ideas.md opens with "DO NOT FIGHT BUT BE CLEVER." and sketches a reversed Rogue: you start at the bottom of a dungeon, and you score only by carrying gold out. docs/osr_crawl.txt wants an OSR-style crawl where ropes and torches matter and fighting is usually a bad idea. docs/quickband.txt copies a Moria-style shop list.

The TODO has a list called "Bring back the concepts from my own game": starting in hell, switching sides, a black horse with movement-based combat, a possessed adventurer's party as enemies. It also collects monster skills worth stealing from Moria, DCSS, Rogue and Brogue. Lower down sit iconic touches I'd still like, such as Cain's "Stay a while, and listen."

Where it started, where it ended

It started as a 7DRL-era sprint in January and February 2024: "first commit", "better light system", "added terminal/ascii mode", "data driven", and then long lists of armor and weapon data. Back then it was my own setting, with Meat Jack, Zedek, Lucifer, a hell town and 12 levels.

After a gap of more than two years, 294 commits landed in September 2026. In them the game became a turn-based Diablo 1 rebuild with D1's tables, formulas, AIs, quests and dungeon generators. About 16k lines of the old H&H story and level templates were deleted along the way. Since then the work has been an autoplay bot to compare pacing against D1, design reviews, and a WebAssembly build with a window manager.

State of the repo

The last commit is from 2026-09-26, and it builds cleanly. All seven milestones in D1_PLAN.md are checked off, from the Cathedral slice through quests and the ending. handover.md describes a branch that swaps D1's ported generators for stand-in ones, with "nothing done on it yet".

design-todo.md has 13 items done and 11 open. The open ones are mostly pacing: H&H still takes about 1.45× D1's walking per level, autoplay isn't fully deterministic per seed, and tactics barely pay off yet. docs/TODO.md still lists gamepad support, an affix cleanup, interesting bosses and all the "my own game" concepts. The Diablo side is there. The part that would make it my game is not, so I'd put it at about 70%.

Is it a real game?

Complete. At least in the D1 mod: there's a town, 16 levels, quests, death with save slots or permadeath, and Diablo on level 16 with an end screen. That's a full Diablo-length run, many hours if you play it straight. The original H&H content is thinner, and its own story was removed. One caveat: after Diablo the end screen opens, but the game carries on behind it.
