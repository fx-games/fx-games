ChainTrapper

Started February 2022 · Go, Ebitengine, Box2D · physics top-down action

Completion ▰▰▰▰▱▱▱▱▱▱ 40% · solid core, campaign and traps missing

▶ Play in browser

ChainTrapper is a small top-down physics game about setting things up and watching them fall over. You play a little red-hooded archer on a map full of zombies, explosive barrels and booster pads, and the fun part is rarely the single shot. It's the chain reaction afterwards, when one barrel takes out the next and the whole row goes up.

I started it in early 2022 as a C# experiment with sticky arrows, wolves chasing sheep and a little map editor. In 2026 I ported the whole thing to Go with Ebitengine and Box2D and finally made it into a game. Arrows stick in enemies and walls, and they get faster and hit harder the longer you draw the bow. You can catch arrows that are still flying by sliding into them at the right moment. You also have a grappling hook, a tether and a few fire traps to place.

The maps are built in the in-game editor (it's on the title menu if you want to poke at it, though saving does nothing in the browser). Each map keeps its own highscore table. In the browser version the highscores only last until you reload the page.

Features

  • Bow with draw-out: arrow speed and knockback scale with how far you pull the stick back (slingshot style), mouse fires at full power.
  • Arrows stick in walls and enemies and drop when the corpse is removed; live arrows can be caught with a timed slide.
  • Exploding barrels that chain into each other, with a combo multiplier and a "most kills with one explosion" stat.
  • Fire and fire traps (up to 3 placed per map), fire sets barrels off, explosions put fires out.
  • Grappling hook and a rope tether, booster pads for slide combos, sliding along walls.
  • Enemies with steering behaviours, paths and a "point of interest" they walk to when they hear an explosion.
  • In-game map editor and six maps in a binary format that still matches the old C# BinaryWriter layout.
  • Per-map highscore table with score and time, keyboard/mouse and gamepad controls, browser (wasm) build.

Under the hood

≈ 3,450 lines of Go · 120 lines of tests · 42 commits

The chain reaction itself, from traps.go: a barrel hit by a blast doesn't explode on the spot, it inherits the cascade and goes off 0.15 s later, which is what makes a row of barrels ripple.

case *ExplodingBarrel:
    if !t.triggered {
        t.chain = b.chain
    }
    t.startExplodeDelayed(0.15)
    combo.KeepAlive()
    continue

Also traps.go, a lesson learned the hard way during the stutter fix:

addShake(8) // no hit-stop: in a cascade repeated freezes read as lag

Explosions are walled: anything behind a wall (checked with a raycast) is safe, and the push falls off linearly with distance, factor := (r - d) / r.

The Go port kept faith with the old maps, from mapfile.go:

// Map uses the same little-endian layout as the C# BinaryWriter version, so existing .bin maps load.

And a port comment in objects.go that explains a no-op honestly:

func (o *Obj) Draw(*Batch) {} // the C# base Draw used an unset DrawScale of 0, i.e. drew nothing

Ideas & inspirations

The README is mostly a German design doc from 2022, written with a friend. "Jürgen's rules" for the first level: you get a bow with three shots and there's one exploding barrel on the enemies' path. Level two adds a second barrel for a chain reaction. That is pretty much exactly what the game became.

My own ideas in there: booster pads on the floor for slide combos, holding onto posts with ropes to slide around corners, and tying enemies up with ropes. The first two made it in.

Much more didn't: falling tree trunks, bear traps, a giant rolling stone, an exploding sheep, "(Heilige Handgranate)", poison, holes with spikes, status effects like wet, oiled, angry or blind, an element system, dive rolls and hiding in grass (those existed in the C# version). There was also a competition idea scoring speed, efficiency and "Chaos & Carnage", and an open questions list that starts with "Welche Fallen gibt es genau?". Early commits had wolves chasing sheep and a shepherd.

Where it started, where it ended

It started on 28 February 2022 in C# / MonoGame / Box2D with a test of sticky arrows shot at a wall, then wolves and sheep, a debug drawer, walls and a map editor with saving and loading. Over March 2022 came steering behaviours, crouching in grass, a dive roll, throwable stones, gamepad support and a slingshot, and it stopped on 29 March with "better slide".

In September 2026 I ported it to Go with Ebitengine and Box2D in one big commit, then spent two days on arrows, catching, draw-out power, explosion feel and a wasm build. It's now an archer-versus-zombies arena with barrels and traps rather than a shepherd game.

State of the repo

Last commit 1 October 2026 (the browser build scripts). It builds, has a smoke test, a map round-trip test and an arrow test. There are no TODO comments in the Go code, but the README TODO list is still from 2022 and the design doc describes a lot that doesn't exist: ten levels, a run where you restart from scratch on death and HP and items carry between maps, resources you craft into traps, and most of the trap list. There are six maps (map04 is missing), played one at a time from a map select. Saving maps does nothing in the browser. Against that plan I'd say it's about 40% there: the core feel is done, the campaign and the trap variety aren't.

Is it a real game?

Playable demo. Each map has a win state (kill every enemy, then a level end screen with score and highscore) and a lose state (you die, press R to retry). There's no campaign or ending, just six maps to clear and beat your own scores on, which is maybe half an hour, longer if you chase highscores or build maps in the editor.
