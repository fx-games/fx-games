Abaddon

March 2024 · 7DRL Challenge · Go + Ebitengine

Completion ▰▰▰▰▰▰▰▰▰▱ 90% · The real jam game, rough edges included

▶ Play in browser7DRL entry on itch.ioSource on GitHub

Abaddon is a quest for revenge. King Kirioth had you banished to the underworld, and now you ride back up out of hell on a demon horse called Black Death, carrying a sword called Vengeance that drains the blood of everything it hits. Your health is finite and never regenerates, so every fight counts. The combat is tactical and leans heavily on the environment: chasms, explosions, knockback.

I made it in seven days for the 7DRL Challenge 2024, on top of my Heaven & Hell engine (Go, Ebitengine and LDtk maps), with Ross Carran contributing to the engine underneath. It placed #36 overall, which I'm still quite happy about.

For a while I believed the jam source was lost, and this page said so. It wasn't: in 2026 I found it on a 7drl branch of the Heaven & Hell repo, committed as "state for 7drl release". The browser version now runs that real jam code, with only the changes needed to build it for the web.

Features

  • Start in the hell town as the Avenger, with Vengeance equipped, the 7DRL quest screens and an epic intro poem
  • A demon horse: press R to mount or dismount, F2 explains the riding maneuvers (Hooves of Hell, Pounding)
  • Vengeance counts the blood it drains, and fights leave blood decals on the floor
  • Chasms, explosions, exploding enemies and lightning as things to use against monsters
  • Five dungeon levels between hell and the Town of Zalem, built from LDtk room templates by an accretion generator
  • A final boss who can't be hurt in his own town and has to be dragged into hell, and a victory screen when he dies
  • Data-driven content: weapons, armor, potions, scrolls, enemies and uniques in recfiles
  • Field of view, light sources, status effects, an earthquake spell, and a terminal mode next to the tile graphics

Ideas & inspirations

The engine's readme.txt says it plainly: an Angband-style crawler "heavily inspired by Angband and the original Diablo", meant to be played in short bursts, with no to-hit rolls, no resistances and no ammo. It even admits: "This won't win any awards in terms of innovation anytime soon".

osr_crawl.txt is an OSR wishlist: low power, combat as a bad idea, at least three options in every situation ("Fight / Flee / Be tricky"), ropes over chasms, torches you can throw. The jam picked up a lot of that: chasms you can knock things into, explosions, and a boss you can't simply out-hit. plan.txt has the juice list (screenshake, hit flashes, camera lerping) and the line "Embrace the grind, but make it short and sweet". The jam's own spoiler screen sums up the boss: "he must be dragged into hell...".

Where it started, where it ended

The first commit is from 2024-01-24, six weeks before the jam. Heaven & Hell started as a Diablo-like with a town above and hell below, a cathedral with Meat Jack on level 2 and Lucifer on level 8. It quickly went data-driven, then got a terminal mode, and in February Ross Carran (29 of the commits) added the earthquake magic.

For the 7DRL I flipped it around: you start in hell, with the quest "Survive the 5 levels of the dungeon and reach the Town of Zalem" and King Kirioth as the man to bring to justice. On top of the engine came the horse and its maneuvers, Vengeance and the blood, chasms, explosions and the ending. It ended as what I shipped on 2024-03-09; the release-state commit followed on 2024-03-20.

State of the repo

The real branch of the abaddon repo is the jam source (2a8ef2e, 2024-03-20) plus three commits from 2026 that make it build for the browser: the terminal title screen and CLI modes moved out of the web build, IPC text servers stubbed, the build and publish scripts. The earlier reconstruction is kept under the tag reconstruction. Compared with the jam binary, all jam-only files and functions are there; only game/root.go was trimmed a little after the build was made.

todo.txt still lists open bugs, like "Word of Recall is instantly", corpses drawn over items, and enchant armor working more than once, and the code is full of "7drl hack" comments and a debug menu that can trigger the win directly. For its own goal, a finished 7DRL entry, it's done. The missing part is polish, not content.

Is it a real game?

Complete (jam-sized). You can die, and since health doesn't regenerate, that happens. You can also win: King Kirioth carries the win_condition tag, a die hook in game/root.go calls onGameWon() when he dies, and that shows the victory text and then the game-over menu. The catch is that he's invulnerable outside the hell town, so you have to get him down there, through chasms. I can see the path in the code but haven't played it through again in the browser. Expect a session or two of rough, hacky jam game with a real ending.
