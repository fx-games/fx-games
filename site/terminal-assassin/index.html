Terminal Assassin

Started August 2023 · Go, Ebitengine · Unicode-grid stealth game

Completion ▰▰▰▰▱▱▱▱▱▱ 40% · deep systems, only one real mission

▶ Play in browserSource on GitHub

Terminal Assassin is what happens when I play too much Hitman and then open a code editor. It is a real-time stealth and assassination game, written in Go on top of Ebitengine, but everything is drawn on a custom Unicode cell grid so it looks like it runs in a terminal.

You get a target, a map full of guards and civilians, and a lot of ways to make things go wrong. Sneak behind people to pickpocket them, knock on walls to lure guards away, change into the clothes of someone you knocked out, poison drinks, expose live power outlets, hide in lockers and drag bodies out of sight. The AI notices blood, missing people and odd noises, and raises the alarm when you get sloppy.

The game has grown into a small sandbox over the years: bows with a pull-back aim, a katana, mines, lamps with dynamic lighting, a fence who buys your loot, a replay recorder and a built-in map editor. Missions come in campaigns, and you can play the first one right here in the browser.

Controls: WASD to move, Shift to run, CapsLock to sneak, arrows to peek, E to interact, Space to use or aim, R to assassinate, Q for the inventory, Esc to pause.

Features

  • Real-time stealth on a Unicode cell grid with field-of-view cones, peeking around corners and dynamic plus baked lighting
  • NPCs on schedules with a state-stack AI that investigates noises, broken windows, bodies and blood, and raises alarms
  • Disguises, pickpocketing, knocking on walls, hiding in lockers, dragging bodies, wiping blood
  • A long item list: pistols, shotgun, sniper rifle with scope, SMG, katana, bows, three kinds of poison, screwdriver, proximity mines, gas grenades
  • Environmental kills: poisoned food and drinks, exposed power outlets, fire, explosions, lingering gas clouds
  • Telegraphed NPC melee with block, parry and dodge
  • Mission briefings with voiced slides, debriefing, career and campaign structure
  • Built-in map editor with tiles, zones, lights, schedules and colours
  • Tick-based replay recorder
  • Full gamepad support with analog aiming and lock-on for thrown items

Under the hood

≈ 36,200 lines of Go · ~210 lines of shell build scripts · 81 commits

Enemy melee attacks come in three colours, and NPCs cycle through fixed patterns of them (src/game/core/melee.go):

MeleeAttackDefault     MeleeAttackType = iota // white – can be blocked and parried
MeleeAttackUnblockable                        // blue  – cannot be blocked
MeleeAttackUnparriable                        // red   – cannot be parried
...
MeleePatternAggressive = MeleePattern{MeleeAttackUnblockable, MeleeAttackUnblockable, MeleeAttackUnparriable}

Every item type is a row in one big traits table, which is how a wrench ends up being both a melee weapon and a throwable (src/game/core/item.go):

ItemTypeSniperRifle: {hasMeleeAction: true, hasRangedAction: true, isRangedWeapon: true, cooldownSecs: 2.00, scopeFoV: 20.0},
ItemTypeSubmachineGun: {..., isAutomaticRangedWeapon: true, cooldownSecs: 0.10},
ItemTypeWrench: {hasMeleeAction: true, hasRangedAction: true, isThrowable: true, isMeleeWeapon: true},

Pathfinding for the AI runs on Dijkstra maps (src/geometry/dijkstra.go), and the briefing screen loops a music track that is literally called "Hitman" (Kevin MacLeod, credited in copyright.txt).

Ideas & inspirations

No secrets here. other/ai.prompts.txt says the game is "heavily inspired by the Hitman and the Thief series". It even has an agency called the ICA, a player called Contractor 23 and a handler named Amy Sterling, codename Nightingale. The first mission is against the Triads (Red Dragon and Blue Lotus).

todo.txt is the real design doc. It starts with four mission types (eliminate, extract, destroy, steal), then a whole section on "Ghost of Tsushima style melee combat" with telegraphs, perfect parries and counter windows. That part actually got built. Further down is a backlog of things I wanted: golf clubs you can hit people in the head with, light switches, guards saying "Keep your distance", a training mission in 14 steps, and a long item taxonomy (triggers, distributions, effects) with environmental hazards like poisoned ventilation and shooting down speakers. other/problems.txt writes down the NPC AI rules in detail, for example that every suspicious spot gets investigated by exactly one NPC, exactly once.

Where it started, where it ended

The first commit in this repo (August 2023) already dropped in about 37,000 lines, including todo.txt and a test procedure, so the actual beginnings happened somewhere else. Then nothing until March 2026, when I came back with a big restructure and almost all the other commits: smooth analog aiming, assassination animations, mines, gas clouds, the replay system, an alarm and AI overhaul, the katana and the melee combat from the todo list. The idea stayed the same the whole time: a small terminal Hitman. What changed is that the sandbox got much deeper while the amount of actual content stayed at one mission.

State of the repo

Last gameplay commit was August 2026 (mines now go off from fire and explosions), followed by web build scripts so it can run here. It compiles. There are only nine TODO comments in the Go code, but todo.txt still lists a "Fix before next release" section and sound bugs like "Snap Neck doesn't have a sound effect". The only real campaign is "first blood" with a single map, Triads-Beta; the rest are test maps. Career stats, challenges, more mission types and the training mission are not there. The systems are maybe two thirds done, the content is barely started, so I'd put it at about 40% of the game I had in mind.

Is it a real game?

Playable demo. There is a real loop: briefing, one mission with a target, you can get killed and fail, and you leave through the exit once the target is dead, followed by a debriefing. But that's one map. Once you've found a few ways to kill the target, maybe an hour or two of fooling around with the toys, it's done.
